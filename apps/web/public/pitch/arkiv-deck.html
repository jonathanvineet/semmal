The problem

Four parties. None of them should have to trust the platform.

Customer

Is this robot any good? Will its record be edited after a bad job?

Robot owner

My robot did the work. Who decides its score?

The robot

An autonomous agent with its own wallet. It answers for its price, hires and results.

Agent developer

My vision agent is listed with a price. Who can delist it or change it?

In Postgres the operator could delete a failed job, edit a score or hide an incident. On Arkiv each record is owned by its author, and readers check that.

Ownership is the permission system

The robot signs its own record. Nobody can alter or withhold it.

Robot PCdevice key generated on the robot, never leaves it, may write only 4 evidence schemas under a daily fee cap
→
Arkiv entity$owner = $creator = robot wallet; body names the same wallet
→
Every reader checksowner and creator match the wallet inside, and that wallet is on the robot's registered profile, or the record is ignored

People sign in their own browser wallet after an in-app approval showing gas and max fee. There is no platform key anywhere.

Scores come from Arkiv, and choose who gets work

One query anyone can run

$ pnpm arkiv:query
task-6f5992c2  COMPLETED  task_score 100
owner 0x103E…29BC (robot wallet)
  Stops completed      40/40  4 of 4 stops
  Valid 0G decisions   20/20  4 of 4 passed
  Findings dealt with  20/20  1 of 1
  Extra stops done       —    not judged
  On time              10/10  before deadline
  agent agt-house-2b5c20 · 4 jobs · 100 each
Indexed task_score → ranking

Robot score = mean of its job scores on Arkiv, minus incidents. Agent score = mean of the job scores the hiring robots signed.

Selection = (judged × score + 3 × 70) / (judged + 3): robots are ranked by it when you book, and agents when a robot hires.

SELECT semmal_episode_v2 ORDER BY task_score DESC. No key needed.
